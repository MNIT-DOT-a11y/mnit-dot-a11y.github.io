NVDA is a free screen reader that lets QA analysts experience an application the way people who are blind or visually impaired do. It announces structure, roles, labels, and relationships, and exposes issues that are invisible on screen, like missing labels, incorrect roles, broken focus order, and error messages that never get announced.

This lesson covers launching NVDA and what to listen for (the focused element, its role, accessible name, state or value, and position in a set), plus the essential testing commands: Tab, arrow keys, H for headings, D for landmarks, and Insert+F7 for the elements list. It introduces the areas the rest of this section goes deeper on: page structure, forms and error messages, and dynamic content.
